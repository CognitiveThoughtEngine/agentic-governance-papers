## Abstract

Individual-level cognitive load measurement (cf. DLI, Paper 1) is necessary but insufficient for understanding how AI-mediated organizations distribute decision burden across their members. This paper extends the Decision Load Index framework to the organizational network level, examining how cognitive load propagates through human-agent decision networks. Drawing on graph-theoretic modeling and empirical data from AI-augmented organizational settings, the paper identifies structural patterns — decision centralization, load concentration nodes, cascade vulnerabilities — that produce burnout and friction at the organizational level even when individual DLI scores appear manageable. The paper derives design principles for AI-mediated organizational architectures: load-balancing through distributed decision authority, circuit-breaking through escalation thresholds, and resilience through redundant decision pathways. These principles map directly to multi-agent system design for autonomous organizations.

---

## Key Findings

- Cognitive load in AI-mediated organizations concentrates at predictable structural nodes — typically the human-AI boundary where oversight decisions accumulate — creating burnout risks that individual DLI measurement does not detect.
- AI-mediated decision networks exhibit cascade failure modes analogous to electrical grid failures: local overload propagates to adjacent nodes faster than human correction can respond.
- Load-balancing design principles derived from network analysis (distributed authority, threshold-based escalation, redundant pathways) reduce organizational DLI concentration by measurable margins in studied cohorts.
